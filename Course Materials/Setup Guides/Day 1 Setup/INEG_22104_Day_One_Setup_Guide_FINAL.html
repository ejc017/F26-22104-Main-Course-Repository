Part 3. Create a GitHub account

GitHub is where you will get starter code, keep your own work backed up, and turn in projects later in the semester. The account is free and stays yours after the course ends.

Create the account

  1. Go to github.com/signup in any browser.
  2. The signup page shows every field at once — email, password, username, and the email preferences question — with a single button at the bottom. Enter your uark.edu email address in the first field.
  3. Create a password. GitHub requires at least 15 characters, or at least 8 characters including a number and a lowercase letter.
  4. Choose a username. It may contain only letters, numbers, and single hyphens. Pick something you would be comfortable showing an employer; a variation on your real name works well.
  5. Answer the email preferences question (either answer is fine) and solve the verification puzzle if you are shown one.
  6. Click Continue. GitHub creates the account and sends your verification code.

Verify your email

  1. GitHub emails you a six-digit code, usually within a minute. If it has not arrived after a few minutes, check your Junk folder.
  2. Type the code into the verification screen. If it expires before you get to it, click the link to send a new one.
  3. GitHub may ask a few setup questions about your interests and plan. You can skip them, and the free plan is all you need — do not pay for anything.

Send me your username

Your username is the last part of your profile address, github.com/your-username, and it also appears at the top of the menu when you click your avatar in the upper-right corner. It is not your email address.

  1. Open the Microsoft Form for INEG 22104 at https://forms.cloud.microsoft/r/ZhnF2VfkiH.
  2. Enter your name and your GitHub username exactly as it appears, including hyphens and capitalization.
  3. Submit the form. A confirmation screen means I have it; you do not need to Slack DM me separately.

Part 4. Claim the GitHub Student Developer Pack

The Student Developer Pack is GitHub's bundle of free professional tools for verified students. Apply on the same day you create your account, because this is the slowest item on the list: GitHub reviews the enrollment document you upload, and while many decisions land within a day or two, some take longer. It also earns you a second way to prove student status to JetBrains in Part 7, which is useful if your uark.edu address gives you any trouble there.

Have one proof of enrollment ready before you start: a photo of your student ID showing a current enrollment date, your class schedule, your transcript, or an enrollment verification letter.

  1. Add your uark.edu address to your GitHub account and verify it, under Settings and then Emails.
  2. Go to github.com/settings/education/benefits and click Start an application.
  3. Fill in the form: name the University of Arkansas as your school, choose Student, and upload your proof of enrollment. Photograph the whole document in good light, since an unreadable image is the most common reason an application comes back.
  4. Submit the application, then watch for GitHub's decision by email. Check your Junk folder if a few days pass in silence.
  5. Once you are approved, open your GitHub Education portal to see what you now have. JetBrains is one of the offers: a free subscription for students, renewed annually.

Figure 2. Verifying your school email first, then applying with a document that proves you are enrolled.

Part 5. Install GitHub Desktop

GitHub Desktop moves your work between your laptop and GitHub without making you type Git commands. It runs on Windows 10 64-bit or later and on macOS 12 or later.

  1. Go to desktop.github.com. The page detects your system and offers the matching download.
  2. Click Download for Windows or Download for macOS.
  3. On Windows, open your Downloads folder and double-click the setup file; GitHub Desktop launches on its own when the install finishes. On macOS, double-click the downloaded zip, then drag GitHub Desktop into your Applications folder and open it from there.
  4. On the welcome screen, choose Sign in to GitHub.com and sign in with the account you created in Part 3. Your browser opens to finish the sign-in and then hands you back to the app.
  5. When it asks for the name and email to attach to your commits, use your real name and your uark.edu address, then click Finish.

Part 6. Install the Liberica JDK 25 (Full)

The JDK is the Java compiler and runtime — the part that turns what you write into a program that runs. We are standardizing on BellSoft's Liberica build of OpenJDK, version 25, in the Full package. The Full package matters: it includes LibericaFX, an implementation of JavaFX for building graphical applications, which the Standard package leaves out.

Before you download, know two things about your computer: whether it runs Windows or macOS, and which processor it has. On a Mac, click the Apple menu and choose About This Mac: any Apple silicon chip — the M-series — means you want the ARM download, while an Intel chip means x86. On Windows, open Settings and look at System, then About. Most Windows laptops report x64 and take the x86 download, but newer Snapdragon machines report ARM and need the ARM one.

  1. Go to bell-sw.com/pages/downloads/.
  2. Select the JDK 25 LTS tab. The page opens on All versions, and there is a JDK 26 tab sitting immediately to the right of the one you want — we are standardizing on 25, so check that 25 LTS is the tab that is selected.
  3. Choose your operating system and then your architecture, using what you looked up above.
  4. In the Package menu, choose Full JDK. This is the step students miss most often — the page opens on Standard JDK.
  5. Download the installer: the MSI file on Windows, the DMG file on macOS.
  6. Run the installer and accept the defaults. On macOS, if you are warned that the file was downloaded from the internet, choose Open; the download came from bell-sw.com.

Figure 3. The settings that have to be right on the download page: version 25, your operating system, your architecture and bitness, and Full JDK.

Check that it worked

Open a new command window. On Windows, press the Windows key, type cmd, and press Enter. On macOS, press Command and the space bar, type terminal, and press Enter. Type java -version, press Enter, then type javac -version and press Enter.

Both commands should answer with a version number that begins with 25. If either one reports that the command is not recognized or not found, close that window and open a brand-new one before trying again.

Figure 4. What a working installation looks like from the command line.

Part 7. Apply for the JetBrains Student Pack

JetBrains gives full-time students free access to its professional development tools for as long as they are enrolled. Approval is often immediate, but it can take up to a business day, so start this step before you sit down to install IntelliJ. If it is still pending on the day you install, go ahead and install anyway — the 30-day trial covers you until the license arrives.

  1. Go to jetbrains.com/academy/student-pack/ and click Apply for Student Pack.
  2. Choose the university email address option. It is the fastest route for us, because JetBrains recognizes our domain and verifies it in minutes. If your uark.edu address is refused for any reason, switch to the GitHub Student Developer Pack option instead.
  3. Enter your uark.edu address, set your status to Student and your country to United States, accept the terms, and submit the form.
  4. Open the confirmation email from JetBrains and click the link inside it.
  5. Accept the license agreement, then create a JetBrains Account using that same uark.edu address, or log in if you already have one.
  6. When you are finished, your JetBrains Account page shows a free educational license covering all of the JetBrains IDEs.

Figure 5. Applying with your university email, and what JetBrains does next.

Two things to keep in mind. The student license covers coursework, personal projects, and academic research, but not commercial work for an employer. And it is renewed annually: JetBrains emails a reminder about two weeks before it lapses, and the renewal link lives in your JetBrains Account. If uark.edu is ever refused as an institution, let me know and verify through the GitHub Student Developer Pack in the meantime.

Part 8. Install IntelliJ IDEA and activate the Ultimate features

Starting with the 2025.3 release, JetBrains ships a single IntelliJ IDEA download instead of separate Community and Ultimate editions. Everyone installs the same file. The Ultimate capabilities — the professional version we want — unlock when you sign in with the student license from Part 7, so there is nothing to purchase and no trial to start.

  1. Go to jetbrains.com/idea/download/ and select your operating system. On macOS, choose the Apple Silicon or the Intel build to match the processor you identified in Part 6.
  2. Download and run the installer. On macOS, drag IntelliJ IDEA into your Applications folder.
  3. Launch IntelliJ IDEA. When the license dialog appears, choose to log in to your JetBrains Account rather than starting a trial, sign in with your uark.edu account, and let the license activate.
  4. Confirm it took: open Help and choose Register, which opens the Manage Subscriptions dialog. It should list a subscription through your educational license rather than a 30-day trial.

Figure 6. Download once, then activate by logging in with the account from Part 7.
